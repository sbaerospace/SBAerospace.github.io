We build rockets.
From scratch.

SBAerospace is a student-run engineering club at SBHS. We compete in the American Rocketry Challenge and build everything ourselves: airframes, electronics, software, and all.

See Our Work About Us
Nasa TechRise Winners SBHS

Built entirely by students.

Every part of our hardware and software is designed, assembled, and tested by SBHS students.

Rocketry

We design custom airframes and fin geometries, utilizing flight simulations to verify stability margins and predict flight paths before manufacturing.

Electronics

Custom PCBs, avionics, and the Rocket++ flight computer, designed in KiCad and programmed in C. We build the hardware that goes inside our vehicles and payloads.

Mechanical

CAD-driven design in SolidWorks and Fusion 360. Structural stress evaluation, composite layups, and 3D printed hardware: everything starts with a drawing.

Software

Flight simulations, ground station GUIs, and tools like CircuitSpace, an open-source circuit simulator our electronics team built and uses in-house.

Engineered from the ground up

Major hardware systems and competition vehicles designed, machined, and flown by our team.

Active Airbrake System

Three servo-actuated flaps that autonomously deploy mid-flight to bleed velocity and hit a precise target apogee. Runs an active PID control loop with Kalman-filtered telemetry.

Rocket++ Flight Computer

Our custom 54mm flight computer built in KiCad and programmed in bare-metal C. Handles state-machine flight detection, dual-deploy pyro ejection, and high-frequency flash logging.

American Rocketry Challenge

Engineered and flown at the National Finals in The Plains, VA. Custom composite airframe designed to hit strict altitude and duration scoring windows with fragile raw egg payloads.

Multiple competitions.
One unified club.

SBAerospace isn't limited to a single vehicle or project. We field competitive teams across national aerospace challenges: from the American Rocketry Challenge (with dedicated physics, simulation, and engineering subteams) to NASA TechRise, Cubes in Space, Genes in Space, and the Drone Challenge.

Members develop specialized skills across CAD, electronics, computational simulation, aerodynamics, and aerospace biology - collaborating across programs all year long.
