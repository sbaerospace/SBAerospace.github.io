South Brunswick High School
Aerospace Club

SBHS Aerospace Club is a student-run club in Monmouth Junction, New Jersey. Members work on rocketry, electronics, CAD, programming, and aerospace experiments through workshops, projects, and competitions.

What members work on

Projects are planned and built by students. Experienced members teach newer members at meetings and on competition teams.

Rocketry

Teams design, build, and fly rockets for the American Rocketry Challenge. Designs are checked in OpenRocket for stability and predicted altitude before anything is built.

Electronics

Circuits built around microcontrollers and sensors, including the airbrake controller for our ARC rocket and the data logger in our NASA TechRise payload.

CAD & Simulation

Parts are modeled in CAD, usually Onshape, and 3D printed. Members also run CFD to see how design changes, such as airbrake flaps, affect airflow.

Programming

Flight code in C++, payload data logging in Python, and CircuitSpace, a browser tool members built for planning rocket wiring.

Recent projects

Each project is labeled with how far it has gotten, from CAD and code to flown hardware.

CAD + flight code

ARC airbrake system

An airbrake module for the 2025–26 ARC rocket. Servo-driven flaps fold out of a 3D-printed airframe section, and our Rocket++ flight code decides when to deploy them to aim for 750 ft.

Prototype

NDVI payload for NASA TechRise

Our TechRise payload measures visible and near-infrared light to map vegetation (NDVI). It logs flight telemetry and sensor readings to an SD card once per second.

Flown

ARC competition rockets

Each season a team builds a rocket to carry a raw egg to the ARC altitude and flight-time targets. Shown: SBHS students’ ARC build on the pad at a World Space Week launch, October 2026.

Membership and
competition teams

General membership is open to all SBHS students. No previous engineering experience is required. Competitive teams have a separate selection process.

For 2026–27, competitive teams are working on the American Rocketry Challenge, NASA TechRise, Genes in Space, Cubes in Space, the Aviation Design Challenge, and the REC Foundation Aerial Drone Competition.
